## whoami

I got into offensive security in 2025. My focus right now is **Active Directory attack paths**, from initial foothold through post-compromise enumeration, lateral movement, and post-exploitation. I'm currently building out my **web application pentesting** skills as the next front, applying what I learn there directly to bug bounty work.

My day job isn't cyber-related yet. I'm actively working the transition into offensive security full-time. No pretense of being a 10-year veteran here. I'm early in the career, deliberate about it, and I document what I learn as I go. If you want someone who treats a pentest like a checklist to tick off, that's not me. If you want someone who actually enumerates before they exploit, keeps notes, and reports clearly, that's what I'm building toward.

## What I actually work with

**Active Directory**
- Initial attack vectors (LLMNR/NBT-NS poisoning, password spraying, misconfigured services)
- Post-compromise enumeration (BloodHound, PowerView, manual LDAP queries)
- Post-compromise attacks (Kerberoasting, AS-REP roasting, pass-the-hash / pass-the-ticket)
- Extended AD attack paths (ACL abuse, delegation abuse, trust relationships)
- Post-exploitation (privilege escalation, persistence, credential harvesting)

**Web Application Security** — *newer ground, actively building this up*
- Working through OWASP Top 10 methodology hands-on
- I use automated tools, but I don't stop at "the scanner flagged it", I dig into *why* something is vulnerable before I trust the result
- Taking what I learn here straight into bug bounty hunting
